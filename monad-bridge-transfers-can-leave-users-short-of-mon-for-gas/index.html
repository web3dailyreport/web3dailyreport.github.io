Monad bridge transfers can leave users short of MON for gas

A monad bridge transfer can deliver an asset without the MON needed to move it again; here is how native gas, source fees and sponsorship differ.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 6, 2026 · 02:49 UTC3 min read

A monad bridge transfer can deliver tokens to a wallet without supplying the MON needed to spend them on Monad. The bridge moves assets between networks; a later transaction on Monad is a separate operation, and the network charges its gas fee in MON.

That distinction matters when planning a transfer. The monad bridge reference belongs in the same decision as the destination balance: check whether the route delivers MON for fees or only the token you intend to use.

Why does a monad bridge transfer not cover gas?

A bridge transaction and a destination transaction happen on different networks. The first pays its fee on the source chain, usually in that chain’s gas token; once the asset arrives, Monad’s own transaction rules apply. Monad documentation names MON as the network’s native gas token.

So a stablecoin balance can be visible and usable in an app while still being insufficient to send, swap or approve that token. Those actions require an onchain transaction, and the wallet needs a way to pay its Monad fee. Bridging a token is not the same as converting part of it into gas.

How can users check whether they have enough MON?

Before confirming a route, inspect both the asset being delivered and the destination fee estimate. A monad bridge route that delivers only USDC or another token may leave the wallet unable to make its next move until MON is available.

  • Check which token the route sends to the Monad address.
  • Review the fee asset and estimate shown by the wallet for the next transaction.
  • Keep some MON available if the route does not include it or another fee arrangement.

The source-chain fee is separate: the wallet must also hold that chain’s gas token to submit the bridge transaction. If the transfer includes a swap or multiple steps, review each displayed fee rather than assuming one estimate covers the entire journey.

When can gas sponsorship cover a Monad transaction?

Some wallets may sponsor eligible fees, but sponsorship has conditions. MetaMask’s published rules, for example, require an eligible Monad-to-Monad transaction and at least 10 MON remaining after the transaction; its help page also lists a 5 million gas ceiling and says hardware-wallet accounts are not supported.

That is a wallet-specific arrangement, not a property of every bridge transfer or token. Check the fee line in the signing screen for sponsorship, and treat it as unavailable if the wallet does not show that the transaction qualifies. For most users, keeping a small MON balance is the straightforward way to avoid having bridged tokens stranded at the next step.
