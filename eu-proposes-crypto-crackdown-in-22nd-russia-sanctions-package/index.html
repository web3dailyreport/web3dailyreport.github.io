EU proposes crypto crackdown in 22nd Russia sanctions package

The Commission’s 22nd sanctions package would cut 60 Russian banks from SWIFT and expand crypto enforcement, including against third-country networks aiding evasion.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 27, 2026 · 12:07 UTC2 min read

The European Commission proposed a new Russia sanctions package on Nov. 23 that would target crypto and payment networks alongside 60 Russian banks, as part of its 22nd package over the war in Ukraine.

The proposal would extend transaction bans to the banks and remove all 60 from SWIFT, the international payments messaging system, according to the Associated Press report on the package. The measures need approval from all 27 EU member countries before taking effect; that process can take weeks and the proposals may change, AP reported.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the crypto measures would focus on Russia and also cover third countries helping it evade sanctions. The Commission’s statement on the package said it had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support enforcement. It did not name specific platforms in the statement.

What the proposal would change

  • 60 banks: The EU would extend transaction bans and remove them from SWIFT, according to von der Leyen.
  • Crypto and payment networks: The proposed crackdown could include networks in third countries if they help Russia evade sanctions.
  • Enforcement: The Commission has launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the work.

The package also proposes barring Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at EU sporting events, and banning official Russian symbols at sports and cultural events, the Commission said. AP reported that the measures are intended to keep economic pressure on Russia; the bloc had already sanctioned more than 4,700 people and entities.

The crypto restrictions remain proposals until the 27 member countries reach agreement. The Commission said it would continue proposing sanctions until Russia shows intent to pursue lasting peace in Ukraine.
