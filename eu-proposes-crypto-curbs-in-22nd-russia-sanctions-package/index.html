EU proposes crypto curbs in 22nd Russia sanctions package

The European Commission proposed its 22nd Russia sanctions package, including crypto and payment-network measures, as the plan awaits approval by all 27 EU states.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 24, 2026 · 17:54 UTC2 min read

On Nov. 23, the European Commission proposed a 22nd sanctions package against Russia, targeting crypto and payment networks and extending transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks, according to the Associated Press report. The proposal must be approved by all 27 EU member states before it can take effect.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the crypto measures would focus on Russia and include third countries that help it evade sanctions. The Commission has also launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that work, according to von der Leyen’s statement.

The proposed financial measures would remove all 60 targeted banks from SWIFT, the international payments messaging system, the Commission said. It did not name the banks in the statement. The proposal also seeks to list more than 70 vessels linked to Russia’s shadow fleet and ban tankers insured in Russia from EU ports.

Other measures would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at sports events in the EU, prohibit official Russian symbols at those events, and restrict financial flows from Russian sponsors to European sports and cultural associations. The Commission also proposed visa restrictions for Russian ex-combatants.

The package is still a proposal. The AP reported that approval by all EU member countries can take weeks and that the measures could change during the process.
