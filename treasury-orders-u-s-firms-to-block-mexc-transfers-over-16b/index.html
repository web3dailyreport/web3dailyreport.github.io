Treasury orders U.S. firms to block MEXC transfers over $16B

FinCEN ordered U.S. financial firms to block transfers to and from MEXC, alleging the exchange handled at least $16 billion in illicit funds over five years.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 8, 2026 · 02:14 UTC2 min read

On Nov. 6, the U.S. Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) ordered U.S. financial institutions to stop transferring funds to or from crypto exchange MEXC Global, alleging the exchange handled at least $16 billion tied to illicit activity. The Treasury announcement said the flows, between May 2021 and May 2026, involved sanctions evasion, cybercrime and other activity.

What transfers will the order block?

The order, issued under Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act, prohibits U.S. financial institutions from transmitting funds to or from MEXC, according to Treasury. It takes effect 30 days after publication in the Federal Register; Treasury said violations may bring civil or criminal penalties.

Treasury said the $16 billion included nearly $1.5 billion linked to Russian sanctions evasion, including $232 million in direct transactions with entities sanctioned by the U.S., European Union and United Kingdom. It said MEXC processed transactions with Russian exchange Garantex and successor Grinex as recently as June 2026.

What did Treasury allege about Iran and North Korea?

Treasury said MEXC sent or received about $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 Iranian crypto exchanges between May 2021 and May 2026. It also cited about $22 million in transactions connected to Iran’s central bank and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, including its Qods Force, and alleged North Korean sanctions evasion.

The order is part of Treasury’s Operation Economic Outcast, which the department described as an effort to disrupt Iran’s access to global finance. Treasury said the legal action rests on MEXC’s alleged Russian illicit finance activity.

How has MEXC responded?

MEXC said it was reviewing the order, continuing to operate normally and conducting an independent review of its anti-money-laundering and sanctions controls, CoinDesk reported. The transfer restrictions take effect after Federal Register publication.
