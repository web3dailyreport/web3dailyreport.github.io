US courts authorize seizure of $1.2 billion in Binance hack crypto

The Justice Department says courts have authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from the Binance hack, as a wider freeze tops $2.7 billion.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 14, 2026 · 00:55 UTC2 min read

U.S. authorities have obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday, Nov. 12. The department said the assets are part of a wider effort that has frozen more than $2.7 billion.

The Justice Department announcement said North Korean military hackers known to the FBI as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion from the exchange on or about Oct. 29. The government and private sector worked with virtual asset service providers to freeze funds.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The Justice Department’s figure for the Binance theft.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: The amount the U.S. government and private sector have frozen so far.
  • More than $1.2 billion targeted by court orders: The portion the department said courts have authorized it to seize or restrain.

The distinction matters: the Justice Department described court authorization to seize or restrain the $1.2 billion, while the broader $2.7 billion figure covers funds frozen by government and private-sector efforts. The Associated Press reported that U.S. authorities typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing, the Justice Department said. TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and the FBI has urged virtual asset providers to block transactions linked to addresses used in the laundering.
