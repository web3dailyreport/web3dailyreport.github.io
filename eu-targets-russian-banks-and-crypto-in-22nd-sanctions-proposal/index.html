EU targets Russian banks and crypto in 22nd sanctions proposal

The European Commission proposed targeting 60 Russian banks and crypto networks in its 22nd sanctions package, which still needs approval from all 27 EU states.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 24, 2026 · 03:12 UTC2 min read

The European Commission proposed targeting 60 Russian banks and crypto networks on Nov. 23, as part of its 22nd Russia sanctions package. The Associated Press report says the package also proposes barring Russian teams and athletes from competing under their flag at events in the EU.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the proposal would extend transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and remove them from SWIFT, the international payments messaging system. The European Commission statement says the measures would also target crypto and payment networks in Russia and in third countries that help Russia evade sanctions.

The Commission did not identify specific crypto platforms or set out a detailed mechanism for the proposed restrictions in its statement. It said it had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the work and align efforts with international partners.

The proposal also includes a ban on Russian teams and athletes competing under the Russian flag at EU sports events. Von der Leyen said the Commission proposed restrictions on Russian symbols at sports and cultural events, Russian sponsorship money going to European sports and cultural associations, and Russians holding positions in European sports federation governing bodies.

The package is a proposal, not yet an enacted sanctions regime. AP reported that all 27 EU member states must endorse it before it can take effect; negotiations could take weeks, and the measures may change.
