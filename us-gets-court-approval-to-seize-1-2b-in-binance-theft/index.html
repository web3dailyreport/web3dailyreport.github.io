US Gets Court Approval to Seize $1.2B in Binance Theft

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from the Binance hack, as efforts to trace and freeze the stolen assets continue.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 19, 2026 · 20:31 UTC2 min read

The U.S. government has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday, Nov. 12. The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor, who stole the assets on or about Oct. 29, according to the Justice Department announcement.

The authorization covers only part of the funds frozen so far, and does not mean the entire amount has been forfeited or returned to victims. The Justice Department said government agencies and private-sector partners had frozen more than $2.7 billion in assets; efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the remaining stolen assets are continuing.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The Justice Department said TraderTraitor took more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The FBI has attributed the theft to North Korean actors.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have blocked this amount so far, limiting how much of the stolen crypto can be moved.
  • More than $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint: A court approved action against this portion of the frozen funds, according to the Justice Department.

The Justice Department said its National Security Division, Criminal Division and the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the assets. The FBI said TraderTraitor actors are continuing to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and urged service providers to block transactions linked to addresses used by the group.

Seizure authorization is a step toward recovery, but the funds’ final disposition remains subject to legal process. The Associated Press reported that U.S. authorities typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department said efforts to trace and forfeit the Binance assets remain ongoing.
