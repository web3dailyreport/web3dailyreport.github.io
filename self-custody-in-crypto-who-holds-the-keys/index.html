Self custody in crypto: who holds the keys

What does self custody mean in crypto for beginners? Learn how private keys, a seed phrase and a small test transfer put you in control of your coins.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 28, 2026 · 01:07 UTC3 min read

Self custody in crypto means you hold the private keys that let you move your funds, usually through a wallet backed up by a seed phrase.

When you buy crypto on an exchange, the exchange typically holds the keys. It can control when withdrawals are available and may freeze them. Your account login gives you access to the exchange’s service; it is not the same as holding the keys to the coins.

With self custody, a wallet such as MetaMask manages your keys on your device. A Ledger hardware wallet keeps them on a separate device. The key is what authorizes a transaction; the seed phrase is a backup that can restore access to the wallet.

How to start holding your own crypto

To start, install a wallet from its official source, then write down its seed phrase on paper and keep it somewhere private and safe. Seed phrases are usually 12 or 24 words under the BIP-39 standard. Anyone who gets the phrase can access the wallet, and there is no customer support that can reset a lost phrase.

Send a small test amount from the exchange to your wallet before moving more. Confirm that it arrives at the right address, then try sending a small amount back if you need to learn the withdrawal process. Check that the network selected on the exchange matches the one supported by your wallet.

Self custody covers control of the keys, not the code or rules of an app you use. Say you deposit from your own wallet into a Morpho vault: Morpho vaults are curated lending pools that allocate deposits across supported markets and yield sources. The vault shares are held in your wallet, so you can redeem them without asking an exchange or custodian; the funds still follow the vault contract’s rules.

That distinction matters: holding your keys lets you authorize an exit, while a smart contract determines how a deposited position works and when it can be redeemed. Read the contract’s terms and make sure you understand the withdrawal process before depositing.

Self custody questions

Is a hardware wallet worth it for a small amount?

For a small amount, a software wallet may be enough to learn the basics. A hardware wallet keeps keys on a separate device, which can suit you as the amount you hold or your security needs grow.

What happens to my crypto if I lose my seed phrase?

If you lose the phrase and cannot access the wallet another way, you may lose access to its funds. Keep a private offline backup and never share it with anyone.

How do I move crypto from an exchange to my own wallet?

Copy the wallet’s receive address, select the matching network on the exchange, and send a small test amount first. Once it arrives, you can transfer more, checking the address and network each time.
