US wins court authority over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department says court orders cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, within $2.7 billion frozen as tracing efforts continue.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 19, 2026 · 06:57 UTC2 min read

The US Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the department said on November 12. The amount is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets frozen through government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department’s announcement.

The theft happened on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets, the department said. The FBI attributed it to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor; its alert on the Binance attack says the group converted stolen assets primarily into ether.

  • More than $1.2 billion: the amount covered by court authorization to seize or restrain. The announcement does not say that all of it has been forfeited or returned to victims.
  • More than $2.7 billion: the total frozen so far by US government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department.
  • More than $6 billion: the value of virtual assets stolen from Binance, according to the FBI and Justice Department.

The legal authorization gives the government a route to pursue control of the assets. The Justice Department said forfeiture efforts are ongoing, and a court would determine who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds, AP reported.

Investigators say TraderTraitor has continued laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation have urged virtual-asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses linked to the theft, the Justice Department said.
