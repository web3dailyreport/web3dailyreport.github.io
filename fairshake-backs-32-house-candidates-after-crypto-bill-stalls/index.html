Fairshake backs 32 House candidates after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake named 32 House candidates for midterm support, with six set to receive $1 million each as the crypto industry's market-structure bill remains stalled.

Blockfront Editorial

Oct 5, 20262 min read

Fairshake announced Monday it will support 32 House candidates in November’s midterms, including six receiving $1 million each, after the Senate blocked a major crypto market-structure bill. The Associated Press report said the slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats, all of whom supported legislation that was a priority for the industry.

Which candidates will get the largest checks?

Fairshake plans to spend $1 million each to support Democratic Reps. Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, and Republican Reps. French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. Roll Call reported that the PAC did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates.

The slate includes incumbents in both parties, among them House Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar and Republican House Majority Whip Tom Emmer. Fairshake spokesman Geoff Vetter said the group backs candidates who support crypto in both parties.

Why is Fairshake spending after the bill stalled?

The Senate blocked the legislation in September on a procedural vote after lawmakers failed to agree on ethics rules addressing President Donald Trump’s crypto investments, AP reported. The bill would have set a federal framework for digital assets markets; the House passed its version in July 2025.

Fairshake is presenting the slate as support for candidates who backed the legislation, as the industry looks to the next Congress to revive it. The issue crossed party lines in the House: all 32 candidates supported the bill, according to AP.

How much more could Fairshake spend before November?

Fairshake and affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash at the end of August, according to AP. Vetter did not say whether the group plans additional spending in House or Senate races before the election.

Fairshake has also pledged nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio, AP reported. The November midterms will determine which members return to Congress as the industry looks to revive its stalled legislation.
