Hong Kong Gazettes Bill to License Crypto Service Firms

Hong Kong gazetted a bill covering crypto dealing, advice, management and custody, requiring SFC licences or registration; lawmakers are due to begin review Dec. 2.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 22, 2026 · 10:42 UTC2 min read

Hong Kong gazetted a bill on Nov. 20 that would require crypto dealing, advisory, management and custody businesses to obtain a licence or registration from the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), according to the government’s announcement. The bill is scheduled for its first reading in the Legislative Council on Dec. 2.

Four virtual asset services enter the proposed regime

The bill would amend Hong Kong’s Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance. Businesses providing any of the four services in Hong Kong would face a fit-and-proper test and other regulatory requirements, the government said.

Banks and stored-value facility licensees overseen by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority would also have to register with the SFC to provide virtual asset services. The proposed framework draws on international standards and Hong Kong’s rules for conventional financial services, according to the government.

Custody rules focus on safeguarding private keys

The proposal follows consultations by the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau and the SFC. In their earlier consultation announcement, the agencies said the custody regime would focus on managing risks from safekeeping clients’ virtual asset private keys in Hong Kong. They also said advisory and management services would have separate licensing regimes.

The agencies reported receiving 101 submissions on dealing services and 93 on custody during a consultation that ended in August 2025. Most respondents supported extending regulation to those services, the agencies said. They launched a further one-month consultation on advisory and management services on Dec. 24, 2025.

Legislative review is next

The bill proposes that the SFC and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority receive powers to implement the licensing and registration regime. SFC chief executive Julia Leung said the proposal would strengthen investor protection and provide clearer routes for responsible innovation, according to the government announcement.

The bill has been gazetted but is not yet law. Its first reading in the Legislative Council is set for Dec. 2.
