US Wins Court Authority Over $1.2B in Binance Hack Funds

The Justice Department won court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion tied to Binance’s $6.3 billion hack; tracing and recovery continue.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 20, 2026 · 18:13 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department said on Nov. 12 it had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in October. The department’s announcement identifies the alleged perpetrators as North Korean military members known to the FBI as TraderTraitor.

The figures show the scale of the theft and the limits of the recovery so far:

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The DOJ said the theft occurred on or about Oct. 29. The Associated Press reported the FBI previously attributed it to TraderTraitor.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have frozen that amount, according to the DOJ.
  • More than $1.2 billion under court authority: That amount is part of the frozen total and is authorized for seizure or restraint, the department said.

The distinction matters: court authorization to seize or restrain funds does not mean the government has completed forfeiture or returned the assets. The Associated Press report says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

U.S. officials and law enforcement partners are working with virtual-asset service providers to trace and block the stolen cryptocurrency. The DOJ said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts remain ongoing.
