US secures authority to seize $1.2 billion in Binance crypto

The Justice Department secured court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion stolen from Binance; efforts to trace and freeze the remaining funds continue.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 16, 2026 · 00:35 UTC2 min read

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in an October 29 attack, the Justice Department said November 12. The department said the theft exceeded $6 billion and attributed it to members of the North Korean military known as TraderTraitor, according to the Justice Department announcement.

The $1.2 billion is part of a larger pool of funds frozen after the breach. The Justice Department said U.S. agencies and private-sector partners had frozen more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets, and that the department had obtained court authorization covering more than $1.2 billion of that amount.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The Justice Department said the attack took place on or around October 29. The FBI identified the suspected perpetrators as North Korean military members called TraderTraitor.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: U.S. government and private-sector efforts had frozen this amount by November 12, according to the department.
  • More than $1.2 billion subject to court authority: The order allows the government to seize or restrain the assets; it does not mean all the funds have been returned to Binance or to victims.

The distinction between frozen and seized matters for what happens next. The Associated Press reported that the Justice Department may file a forfeiture claim before a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Authorities are still tracing the stolen assets. The Justice Department said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder them through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the funds are ongoing. The Associated Press report also noted that the government’s court authorization covers only part of the total already frozen.
