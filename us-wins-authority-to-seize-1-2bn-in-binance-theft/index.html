US wins authority to seize $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department secured court authority to seize or restrain $1.2 billion of Binance theft proceeds, part of a wider $2.7 billion freeze as recovery work continues.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 23, 2026 · 00:45 UTC2 min read

The Justice Department said on Nov. 12 it had court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in a theft attributed to North Korean hackers. The department’s announcement said the funds were part of a larger effort to block the proceeds.

The figures describe different stages of recovery, according to the Justice Department and the Associated Press:

  • More than $6 billion in virtual assets were stolen from Binance on or about Oct. 29, according to the Justice Department. AP reported the theft at about $6.3 billion.
  • U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far, the department said.
  • More than $1.2 billion of that frozen amount is covered by court authorization to seize or restrain it. That authorization does not mean the full amount has been returned to victims.

The FBI identifies the North Korean military hackers as TraderTraitor. The Justice Department said the hackers have continued laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, while authorities and virtual asset service providers work to trace and block them.

The Associated Press report said the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge in such cases, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets are ongoing.
