Chainalysis says breach exposed tokens for all users

Chainalysis says an October intrusion exposed account data and live tokens for every user; its services went offline as it investigates and restores customer access.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 13, 2026 · 11:57 UTC2 min read

Chainalysis said on Nov. 4 that names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all its users had been exposed to a malicious actor. The company’s security incident update said its investigation had found the exposure on Monday, two days before it disclosed the findings.

The update followed an Oct. 30 notice that said Chainalysis had found no evidence of unauthorized access to credentials or session tokens. Chainalysis said an external actor had used “offensive agentic techniques” to gain access to its systems and internal tools. Ars Technica’s report on the breach described the later disclosure as a broader account of what was exposed.

In a separate Nov. 4 notice, Chainalysis said an “on-path attack” had exposed passwords belonging to a subset of customers, and that it had contained and fixed that attack method. The company has not publicly specified how many passwords were involved. Its broader disclosure lists password hashes and live tokens for all users; it does not say that clear-text passwords or every user’s password were exposed.

Chainalysis took Reactor and Data Solutions offline while investigating. It later took its KYT transaction-monitoring interface and API, Address Screening products and login page offline after detecting abnormal activity. The company said it was directly notifying customers whose KYT accounts appeared to have been accessed without authorization.

The company said it had no evidence that investigation data was accessed or its attribution data manipulated. It required users to reset passwords and rotate API keys as applicable before registering transactions, and asked customers to submit trusted IP addresses for account access.

Chainalysis said Reactor, KYT and Address Screening returned online on Nov. 7 with enhanced security measures. Data Solutions came back online on Nov. 10, the company said, as it continued restoring customer access.
