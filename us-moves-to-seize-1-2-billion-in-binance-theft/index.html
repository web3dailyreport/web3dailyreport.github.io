US moves to seize $1.2 billion in Binance theft

The Justice Department says court-authorized action covers more than $1.2 billion of Binance funds, part of $2.7 billion frozen after an October theft.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 15, 2026 · 02:17 UTC2 min read

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in an October 29 theft of more than $6 billion, the Justice Department said Thursday. The department’s announcement says the action is part of efforts to recover assets attributed by the FBI to North Korean hackers known as TraderTraitor.

The $1.2 billion is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen funds that U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have frozen so far, according to the department. Court authorization covers the portion that authorities can seize or restrain; the announcement does not say that the full amount has been forfeited or returned to Binance.

The Justice Department said the hackers continued to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges as investigators worked to trace and freeze them. Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to move the stolen Binance funds.

The theft occurred on or about October 29, according to the Justice Department. The FBI has attributed it to TraderTraitor, which the department identifies as members of North Korea’s military.

The Associated Press reported that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim before a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing.
