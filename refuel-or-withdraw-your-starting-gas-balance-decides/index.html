Refuel or withdraw? Your starting gas balance decides

Gas refuel vs withdrawing gas from an exchange for a new chain: use refuel when bridging with source gas; withdraw when every wallet holds zero native token.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 28, 2026 · 06:55 UTC3 min read

Use gas refuel when you are already bridging from a chain where you hold gas; use an exchange withdrawal when you hold no native gas on any chain. Refuel adds destination gas to a transfer, but the bridge transaction still needs source-chain gas.

Say you hold USDC and ETH on Ethereum and want to move to Base. A Jumper bridge transfer can include a little ETH on Base, so you can make a follow-up transaction after arrival; using Jumper to bridge changes whether you need a separate gas-funding step. LI.FI’s documentation says the user pays the source-chain transaction gas and the bridge collects some of the transferred tokens for destination gas.

Do you have gas on the chain you’re bridging from?

Choose refuel when you have source gas and are making the bridge transfer anyway. In the Ethereum-to-Base example, ETH pays for the Ethereum transaction, while the transfer delivers a small amount of ETH on Base alongside the USDC. You do not need a separate exchange withdrawal for that initial Base gas.

That source balance is the deciding condition: refuel is part of a paid transaction, not a way to start from a wallet with no gas anywhere. Jumper bridge aggregates routes across supported bridges and swaps, and can add a small amount of destination native gas to a transfer. The amount is meant to get you started; check that it covers the next transaction you plan to make.

Does your exchange support the destination network?

Choose an exchange withdrawal when every wallet balance holds zero native token. It does not require gas on another chain, so it is the way in when you cannot pay for a bridge transaction. Coinbase and Binance let you select a withdrawal network where the asset supports multiple networks; Coinbase Help lists both Arbitrum and Base among supported USDC networks.

Compare the withdrawal’s fee and minimum with the small amount of gas you need. Exchanges charge a flat withdrawal fee, and the minimum can exceed what a first transaction needs, so you may have to send more than the gas amount alone. That can still be the practical route when there is no source gas to fund a bridge.

Match the selected network to the wallet’s destination chain. Coinbase Help tells users to choose the network associated with the receiving address; choosing another network can send funds somewhere other than the intended destination. Network support also depends on the asset and exchange, so confirm it before submitting.

Choose refuel if you have source gas and are bridging anyway; otherwise, use an exchange withdrawal that supports the destination network, then send only what its minimum and fee allow.
