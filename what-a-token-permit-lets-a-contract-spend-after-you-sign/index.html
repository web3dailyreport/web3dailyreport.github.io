What a token permit lets a contract spend after you sign

What is a permit signature when approving tokens? It lets a named spender transfer up to an approved token amount, subject to its allowance and deadline.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 28, 2026 · 04:45 UTC2 min read

An ERC-2612 token permit lets a named spender transfer up to the approved amount of a token from your wallet. The signature sets an allowance once it is submitted onchain, so the spender can use that permission through ERC-20 transfers.

That permission can appear when an app asks you to approve token use without first sending a separate approval transaction. A USDC bridge is one place this question comes up: it moves USDC from one blockchain to another.

What does a permit signature authorize?

An ERC-2612 permit authorizes an allowance for a specific token and spender. Its key parts are:

  • Token and chain
  • Named spender
  • Allowance amount
  • Signature deadline

The signature is created offchain, so signing it alone does not record the allowance onchain. Someone must submit the permit in a transaction and pay gas; the permit can be submitted alongside the transaction that uses the allowance. ERC-2612 checks the signature, deadline and nonce before setting the allowance.

Take a permit for 100 USDC naming a USDC bridge contract as spender. That spender may transfer up to 100 USDC from your wallet, including in several transfers whose total stays within the allowance. ERC-20’s transferFrom function is the standard mechanism for spending tokens with an allowance.

What changes when the deadline passes?

The deadline limits when someone can submit the signed permit. Once submitted, the resulting allowance can remain usable after that deadline; the deadline does not itself revoke permission already recorded onchain.

For a bridge transaction, the spender controls transfers within the allowance, including where the tokens go. Before signing, match the token and chain to the transaction you expect, then check the spender, amount and deadline. A familiar token name does not identify the spender: read the contract or account named in the permit.

ERC-2612 is one permit format. Permit2 and other signature schemes can use different permission rules, so the same assumptions may not apply. Review the specific token, spender and signed details before approving.
