US seeks forfeiture of Binance hack funds after $1.2B seizure

The FBI says a court authorized action against $1.2 billion from Binance’s $6.3 billion hack; a new forfeiture case covers a $1 billion USDC portion.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 24, 2026 · 04:44 UTC2 min read

The FBI said on Nov. 12 that US authorities had court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency reported stolen from Binance in an Oct. 29 hack. The Associated Press reported that the action is part of a wider effort that has frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds.

A Nov. 23 Justice Department filing starts a civil forfeiture case over a portion of the assets, valued at about $1 billion. The Justice Department’s forfeiture complaint says the case covers USDC held across blockchain addresses linked to the theft.

  • $6 billion: The complaint says hackers accessed about this much in virtual currency from Binance. Binance told law enforcement that the breach most likely began when an employee downloaded malware sent during a purported job interview.
  • About $1 billion: The assets in the civil case are USDC, a dollar-pegged stablecoin issued by Circle. The filing says Circle blocked access to the addresses, but the US government cannot transfer the tokens from them.
  • More than $2.7 billion: The AP reported that the US government and private-sector partners had frozen this amount in total. The $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization is part of that broader figure.

The US Secret Service, FBI and IRS Criminal Investigation assess that North Korean military hackers known as Lazarus Group or APT38 carried out the Binance theft, according to the complaint. The complaint says Circle has agreed to issue an equivalent amount of USDC to the United States if the court orders forfeiture.

The government is asking the court to forfeit the assets. Circle’s proposed transfer depends on a final forfeiture order; the complaint does not say that the US has already taken custody of the tokens.
