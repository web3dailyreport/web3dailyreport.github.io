Binance secures $6.5 billion freeze after crypto theft

Binance says an Abu Dhabi court issued a worldwide $6.5 billion freeze tied to its October hack, while separate orders give partners a legal route to return assets.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 20, 2026 · 04:31 UTC2 min read

Binance said on Nov. 18 that Abu Dhabi’s ADGM Courts issued a worldwide freezing order covering $6.5 billion in connection with the Oct. 29 theft from its wallets, according to The Block’s report. The order targets unidentified people accused of carrying out the attack, and the figure is the amount covered by the injunction, not a confirmed recovery.

What do the court orders allow?

The Block reported that Justice William Stone issued the worldwide order and that the court also granted separate interim orders over specific stolen assets, including orders requiring assets to be handed over. Binance said the orders can be served onchain and provide a legal route for partners to return funds they have frozen.

Binance’s lawyers identified 26,000 wallet addresses holding stolen assets, The Block reported, and said the list would be updated as funds move. Binance said it continues to work with law enforcement, issuers, exchanges and blockchain intelligence firms to trace and recover the assets.

Which companies must keep assets frozen?

A separate order applies to 18 companies, including Circle and World Liberty Financial, and requires them to keep specific stolen tokens frozen pending further proceedings, Reuters reported, citing court documents. The companies are barred from lending, swapping or mixing the assets; the court said they were not accused of wrongdoing.

Binance previously said about $2.85 billion of the roughly $6.34 billion affected by the theft had been frozen or intercepted by third parties. Tether, which said it had frozen $1.5 billion, was not included in the order and said it would work with Binance on returning tokens, Reuters reported.

What happens next?

The court is scheduled to consider on Dec. 1 whether the proprietary injunctions should continue and on what terms, Reuters reported. Binance said it had absorbed the financial impact of the theft and made users whole, while continuing to pursue recovery of the stolen assets.
