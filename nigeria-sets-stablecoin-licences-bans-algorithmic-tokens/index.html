Nigeria sets stablecoin licences, bans algorithmic tokens

Nigeria’s central bank set separate licences for naira and foreign-currency stablecoins, barred algorithmic tokens and gave operators 180 days to apply.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 8, 2026 · 00:06 UTC2 min read

Nigeria’s Central Bank introduced licensing rules for stablecoin issuers and other non-security virtual asset providers on Nov. 5, requiring token approval and barring algorithmic stablecoins. The rules create a regulatory framework for tokens used in payments and settlement, according to the CBN’s regulations for non-security virtual assets.

Which stablecoin licences does the CBN require?

The rules set up separate issuer licences for naira-referenced Class 1 stablecoins and foreign-currency-referenced Class 2 stablecoins. Issuers must also receive written approval for each token before issuing it, covering its classification, reference currency, reserves, name, ticker and supported blockchain networks, according to The Block’s report on the rules.

Foreign-currency stablecoins offered to Nigerians must be issued through a Nigerian-incorporated entity with a Class 2 licence. The CBN says the rules apply to covered activities conducted in Nigeria, directed at Nigerian users or affecting the country’s payments system, financial sector or foreign-exchange market, even when the operator has no physical presence in the country.

Which tokens are prohibited?

The regulations prohibit stablecoins that rely solely or primarily on algorithmic mechanisms to maintain their value without matching reserve assets. They also bar fiat-referenced tokens backed mainly by other virtual assets, tokens linked to sanctioned persons or jurisdictions, and tokens marketed as stablecoins without an enforceable right to redemption at par.

The CBN says issuers must receive eligible reserve assets before minting tokens; minting against expected future receipts is prohibited. Issuers must publish a CBN-approved white paper before issuance, with disclosures including the token’s backing, redemption rights and risks.

When must existing operators apply?

The rules took effect on Nov. 5, 2026. Existing operators have 180 days to apply for the required licence or authorisation, subject to the regulations’ transitional requirements, The Block reported.
