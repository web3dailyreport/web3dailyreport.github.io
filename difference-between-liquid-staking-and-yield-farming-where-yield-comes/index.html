Difference between liquid staking and yield farming: where yield comes from

Liquid staking vs yield farming differences come down to the source of returns: staking rewards versus fees and emissions, with different risks, exits and upkeep.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 28, 2026 · 03:31 UTC3 min read

Choose liquid staking for staking rewards on one asset; choose yield farming for trading fees and farm emissions from supplied liquidity. The yield source sets the risks and work: farming adds impermanent loss and reward-token price risk, while staking keeps exposure simpler.

What changes when you move to a farm

Liquid staking pools ETH for Ethereum validation and issues a claim token such as Lido’s stETH or Rocket Pool’s rETH; running a validator directly requires 32 ETH. Staking rewards accrue in the staked asset: stETH rebases its balance, while rETH and wstETH reflect rewards through a rising token value.

Yield farming supplies tokens to a liquidity pool, where trading fees and sometimes emission tokens pay providers. Emissions are split pro rata among liquidity providers, so your share depends on how much liquidity you contribute relative to the pool.

That changes what a quoted farm yield is worth. Staking rewards arrive in the staked asset itself; farm emissions often arrive in a separate token that you may need to sell. If that token’s price falls, the value of your reward falls too, even when the farm distributes the expected number of tokens.

Farming also adds impermanent loss: changes in the relative prices of the assets in a pool can leave your position worth less than holding those assets separately. Both routes carry smart-contract risk, and a farm can involve several contracts and tokens. The Beefy app is a multichain yield optimizer that automatically compounds rewards from supported DeFi strategies; before depositing, Beefy app token approval limits matter because an approval sets how much of a token a contract may access.

Exits take different steps. With liquid staking, you can sell the liquid staking token or redeem it through the protocol, subject to its available process and timing. From a farm, you remove liquidity first, then sell any reward tokens separately; pool prices and token prices can affect the amount you receive.

Which option fits

Liquid staking suits someone seeking one-asset exposure with little upkeep. Its return comes from network staking rewards, and its exit is generally a sale or redemption of the liquid staking token.

Yield farming suits someone willing to manage a liquidity position and accept impermanent loss and emission-token price risk for the possibility of higher yield. Compare the fee and emission sources, the pool’s asset mix, and the reward token before entering.

A vault that farms a liquid staking token combines the routes: staking rewards accrue to the LST while the vault seeks extra fees or emissions from an LST pool. That adds pool, vault and reward-token risks to staking exposure. Choose it when the added yield source is worth those extra moving parts; otherwise, liquid staking alone is the simpler fit.
