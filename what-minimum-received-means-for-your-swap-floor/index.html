What Minimum Received Means for Your Swap Floor

What does minimum received mean in a crypto swap? It is the lowest output accepted before a swap reverts, set by the quote and your slippage tolerance.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 28, 2026 · 06:40 UTC2 min read

A swapper reading a quote can treat minimum received as the smallest output the swap will accept before it reverts.

How is minimum received calculated?

Minimum received is the quoted output reduced by your slippage tolerance: expected output × (1 − slippage tolerance). If execution would pay less than that floor, the swap reverts, and the gas spent on the transaction is not returned.

Take a quote for 1,000 USDC with 0.5% slippage tolerance: the minimum received is 995 USDC. A higher tolerance lowers the floor, so fewer price changes cause a failed swap, but you accept a smaller minimum output.

Price impact is the estimated effect of your trade on the asset’s price; slippage is the change between the quoted price and execution. The minimum received figure turns your slippage tolerance into an output floor you can compare across quotes.

On a cross-chain quote, the destination swap can be the step that determines whether the route meets its minimum. Jumper bridge shows the minimum for the destination token after the route’s steps; use the Jumper bridge quote to check that floor before approving the transfer.

If the final swap cannot meet its minimum, many routes can still deliver the bridged intermediate token, such as USDC, instead of reverting the entire transfer. Read the route details to see what asset could arrive if that destination swap does not complete.

What should you check next?

What is price impact in a swap?

Price impact is the estimated change to a token’s price caused by the size of your trade. It can affect the quote before execution; slippage tolerance sets how much the execution price may move from that quote.

Can I get more than the expected amount?

Yes. The expected amount is an estimate, and a more favorable execution can deliver more. Minimum received sets the lower threshold, not a cap.

What is a sandwich attack?

A sandwich attack places trades before and after yours to move the price against your swap. Your minimum received can cause the swap to revert if the resulting output falls below the floor.

Do I get gas back if a swap fails?

No. A reverted swap does not complete the exchange, but the network still charges gas for processing the transaction.
