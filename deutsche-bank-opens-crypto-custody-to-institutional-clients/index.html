Deutsche Bank opens crypto custody to institutional clients

Deutsche Bank has opened crypto custody to paying institutional clients in Europe and agreed with Deutsche Asset Management to explore digital asset products.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 11, 2026 · 18:07 UTC2 min read

Deutsche Bank said on Nov. 11 that its digital asset custody service is live for paying clients, giving corporate and institutional customers in Europe a way to hold and transfer selected crypto assets through the bank. The launch came with a memorandum of understanding with Deutsche Asset Management to explore digital asset collaboration, according to The Block’s report on the launch.

Which assets can clients hold, and how does custody work?

The service supports bitcoin, ether, USDC, EURC and EURAU, The Block reported. Deutsche Bank manages the clients’ wallets and private keys, and clients can transfer assets to other parties. The bank’s September announcement said the service is aimed at eligible corporate and institutional clients in Europe, including asset managers, hedge funds, custodians, brokers and sovereign institutions; it is not intended for retail clients.

What will Deutsche Bank and its asset manager explore?

The memorandum covers possible work on tokenized money market funds, crypto-asset exchange-traded commodities and stablecoins, The Block reported. The firms will examine custody and settlement, liquidity management, treasury and distribution using Deutsche Bank’s custody platform. The agreement sets out areas to explore; it does not announce a joint product launch.

What comes next for the service?

Deutsche Bank’s September release said the asset range could expand, subject to client demand and the bank’s approval, risk and regulatory processes. Paul Maley, the bank’s global head of trust and securities services, said the medium-term aim is an integrated regulated system for digital asset custody, tokenization and settlement. The bank has not announced a timetable for the products covered by the memorandum.
