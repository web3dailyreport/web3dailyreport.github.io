What is a gas fee in crypto swaps: Read the network charge

A gas fee in crypto swaps is the network charge for processing your transaction, paid in the chain’s native token and separate from the tokens you trade.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 27, 2026 · 13:05 UTC2 min read

A gas fee is the network’s charge for processing your swap, paid in the chain’s native token and separate from the swap price. Each onchain transaction uses computation, measured in gas units, and the network charges a price for each unit.

On Ethereum, EIP-1559 splits that price into a base fee and a priority fee. The base fee is burned, while the priority fee goes to the validator who processes the transaction. Together, they set the price per gas unit.

  • Gas units measure the computation a transaction uses.
  • The price per unit is expressed in gwei.
  • The chain’s native token pays the resulting fee.

Multiply the gas units by the price per unit to estimate the fee in native token. One gwei is 10-9 of the native token, so 150,000 gas at 20 gwei costs 3,000,000 gwei, or 0.003 ETH.

The unit price can rise when the network is congested, as the base fee increases. The gas units can rise with a more complex route. Layer 2 networks and Polygon charge far less per unit than Ethereum, though the total fee still depends on the transaction.

For a first swap of a token, you usually make two transactions: approve the app to use the token, then submit the swap. Each transaction uses gas, so budget for two network fees. Later swaps may not need another approval, depending on the token and approval already granted.

Matcha swap finds routes across liquidity sources, and its estimate shows the network fee before you confirm. The Matcha swap page adds context on how those routes fit into a token exchange. Your wallet pays the fee in ETH, POL or the native token of the chain you use.

Before confirming, check that your wallet has enough of that native token for the estimate, including any approval transaction. This avoids starting a first swap without the balance to cover both fees.
