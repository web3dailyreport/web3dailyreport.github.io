Omnibridge Ethereum Claim Gas Requirements

Why is my Omnibridge Ethereum claim gas estimate so high? Fixed-gas checks can demand headroom beyond gasUsed, while Ethereum fee rates shape the cost preview.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 20, 2026 · 18:11 UTC3 min read

A 1,000,000-gas allowance sets a 0.03 ETH ceiling in this hypothetical Ethereum claim, even if its receipt later shows 200,000 gasUsed. That gap can come from the claim’s required gas limit and Ethereum’s fee market, which affect different parts of the preview.

  • Separate the gas limit required for execution from the fee cap used to calculate the maximum cost.
  • Trace the claim through signature verification, gas forwarding and token release.

Why can the receipt use less gas than the estimate?

An Omnibridge claim may verify a signed message, call a mediator and release tokens through several contracts. The simulation must allow enough gas for that full path to execute successfully.

On fixed-gas Arbitrary Message Bridge (AMB) paths, the MessageProcessor source checks that enough gas remains to forward the configured amount under EIP-150. That headroom check can require a transaction limit well above the gas ultimately consumed.

Gnosis Bridge’s manual-claim instructions say MetaMask can show high estimates while actual consumption is often lower. The estimate is a requirement for the attempted execution; the receipt’s gasUsed records what the successful transaction consumed.

What happens if you retry with gasUsed?

Suppose a successful receipt shows 200,000 gasUsed, but the fixed-gas claim path needs a 1,000,000-gas allowance to pass its forwarding check. Retrying with a 200,000 gas limit may fail during simulation: after the contract’s own work, EIP-150 leaves too little gas to forward the required amount.

That is why gasUsed is not automatically a safe retry limit. Keep the allowance required for the claim path; reducing it to match a prior receipt can make the claim fail before the mediator or token release finishes.

The maximum cost is a separate calculation. At a 30 gwei fee cap, 1,000,000 gas gives a 0.03 ETH ceiling; if the transaction uses 200,000 gas at a 20 gwei effective price, the paid amount is 0.004 ETH. Ethereum’s fee mechanics distinguish the gas limit and fee cap from gas used and the effective price charged.

RPC data and Ethereum execution also serve different roles in a cross-chain claim. The gnosis chain RPC is a concrete example of the endpoint used to read Gnosis Chain state in the Gnosis Bridge flow; the claim’s execution allowance applies on Ethereum.

A high Omnibridge estimate can therefore reflect EIP-150 forwarding headroom, while the final charge follows actual gasUsed and the effective fee price. Check both before treating the preview ceiling as the amount the claim will cost.
