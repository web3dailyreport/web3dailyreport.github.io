Transfer tax before swapping: How to check a token

To check if a token has a transfer tax before swapping, inspect its contract and simulate the exact trade to estimate the amount you’ll receive before signing.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 19, 2026 · 13:05 UTC3 min read

Inspect the token contract and simulate the exact swap before signing, then compare the quote with the amount its fee rules imply you will receive. A token can deduct a fee on transfers, and those rules may differ for buys, sells, amounts, or wallets.

Say you want to swap Token A for Token B. A quote may show an estimated output, but a transfer fee can reduce the amount that reaches you. Start by identifying the exact token and network so you inspect and simulate the same asset you plan to trade.

What should you inspect in the token contract?

Find the token’s contract address on the intended network and open that full address in a block explorer. Match every character: a token name or symbol alone may not identify the contract you hold.

When the explorer shows verified source code, look for transfer logic and fee settings. These may show deductions on buys or sells, different rates by amount, or exemptions for particular wallets or addresses.

For a token with transfer fees, use KyberSwap fee-on-transfer swap settings as a practical reference when preparing the trade. On KyberSwap, select the same network and token pair you intend to swap, then compare the available quote or simulation with the fee rules you found.

An unverified contract makes those rules harder to assess from the explorer. In that case, a quote alone may leave uncertainty about which transfers incur a fee.

How can a simulation estimate the amount you will receive?

Simulate the intended swap with the same token pair, network, and trade amount, then compare its estimated output with the contract’s fee behavior. A simulation reflects its inputs and the state at the time it runs; execution conditions can change before the swap is processed.

Pay attention to each transfer in the route. A swap can involve the token moving from your wallet to a pool, and a routed trade may pass through more than one pool before the output reaches you. If the token charges a fee on multiple legs, each deduction can lower the next leg’s input, compounding the reduction in the final amount.

Buy and sell rules can also differ, so a fee observed for one direction may not describe the reverse swap. Compare the simulated output with what the relevant fee rules imply for the exact direction and amount you chose.

Use that comparison to estimate what will arrive and decide whether to proceed. The contract inspection explains likely fee conditions; the exact-trade simulation shows their effect on the current route and state.
