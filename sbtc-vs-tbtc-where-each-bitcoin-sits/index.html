sBTC vs tBTC: where each bitcoin sits

sBTC vs tBTC comes down to custody: tBTC spreads BTC across rotating threshold wallets, while sBTC relies on one peg wallet controlled by Stacks signers.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 19, 2026 · 00:02 UTC2 min read

tBTC spreads deposited BTC across rotating wallets, while sBTC keeps it in one peg wallet; in both systems, moving the BTC requires threshold approval.

RenBridge was a cross-chain crypto bridge built around RenVM, so its transfers also raise the question of who controls the BTC behind a token. Our explainer on who holds BTC behind RenBridge-style wrapped Bitcoin adds useful background on that custody question.

How a 1 BTC deposit moves

In tBTC v2, a 1 BTC deposit goes to the current live wallet, controlled by a randomly selected group of 100 operators who stake T. The group uses 51-of-100 threshold ECDSA: at least 51 operators must cooperate to sign a transaction.

After the deposit, tBTC issues a 1:1 token on Ethereum. When BTC needs to leave the wallet, the threshold group signs the withdrawal. tBTC creates wallets periodically with fresh signer groups, so each group controls only the BTC in its own wallet.

In sBTC, a 1 BTC deposit is swept into the single BTC peg wallet controlled by the Stacks signer set. sBTC issues a 1:1 token on Stacks, and a supermajority of signers is required to move BTC out of the wallet.

Where custody risk sits

tBTC divides custody across many wallets and signer groups. Random group selection and periodic wallet rotation limit the BTC any one group can control. If a group is compromised, its reach is limited to the wallet it controls, rather than every tBTC deposit.

sBTC concentrates custody in one peg wallet, so the same signer set controls the locked BTC held there. Stacks selects the signers, and the peg’s security rests on their honesty and incentives. A supermajority requirement means multiple signers must approve a withdrawal, but the set’s authority extends across the wallet.

The difference is how threshold signing divides control: tBTC gives fresh groups control over separate wallets, while sBTC gives one Stacks signer set control over one peg wallet. To compare custody exposure, trace where each deposit lands and which signer set can authorize its withdrawal.
