Visa adds Monad as a stablecoin settlement option

Visa added Monad as a stablecoin settlement option on Nov. 10, giving participating issuers and acquirers another blockchain for settling obligations.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 11, 2026 · 04:24 UTC2 min read

Visa added the Monad blockchain to its stablecoin settlement program on Nov. 10, giving participating card issuers and acquirers another network for settling obligations. The Monad Foundation announcement says the option lets partners settle with Visa in stablecoins over Monad.

Visa Vice President Dan Roesbery said the addition expands the networks available to partners as Visa builds multi-chain settlement infrastructure. The announcement did not name which stablecoins can settle on Monad or identify participating issuers and acquirers, according to Unchained’s report.

How will issuers and acquirers use Monad?

Participating issuers and acquirers can use Monad to settle Visa obligations in stablecoins, according to the Foundation. It says that using stablecoins can let funds move over weekends and holidays, while Monad’s Ethereum Virtual Machine compatibility may allow partners already using Ethereum custody, wallet and compliance tools to extend those integrations to Monad.

The Foundation says Monad reaches transaction finality in 800 milliseconds. The network is also described as permissionless, meaning participants do not need approval to access it. The announcement says settlement happens behind the scenes, leaving the Visa experience for merchants and cardholders unchanged.

What does the $25 billion figure measure?

Visa reported a $25 billion annualized stablecoin settlement run rate as of September 2026, roughly 10 times the year-earlier figure, alongside more than 225 stablecoin-linked card programs live globally, according to the Foundation’s release. The run rate is a Visa-wide measure; the announcement does not say that $25 billion was settled on Monad.

The release also says Visa partners are expected to get Monad’s Private Settlement functionality in the coming months. It is designed to keep commercially sensitive details confidential while allowing institutions to share them with auditors and regulators; Unchained reported that the feature is not live yet.
