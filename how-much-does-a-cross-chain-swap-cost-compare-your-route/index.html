How much does a cross-chain swap cost? Compare your route

How much does a cross-chain swap cost in fees? There is no fixed total: source gas, bridge or relayer fees, destination gas, pool fees and price impact all count.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 28, 2026 · 11:27 UTC3 min read

After a transfer quote appears, the cost of a cross-chain swap is the source-chain gas, bridge or relayer fee, destination gas, and pool fees plus price impact on each swap leg. There is no fixed total because each leg is priced by a different chain and provider.

A cross-chain quote combines costs from both chains

A route can swap tokens on a decentralized exchange (DEX) on the source chain, move value across the bridge, then swap again on the destination chain. The source transaction uses gas; each DEX leg may add a pool fee and price impact, while the bridge or its relayer charges for moving the assets.

Relayers usually pay destination gas in advance, then deduct that cost from the output. This means the same small transfer can lose a much larger share to destination gas on Ethereum mainnet than on an L2, where gas costs are often lower. The deduction is part of the route’s total even when it is not presented as a separate charge.

Compare providers by the final amount received, not by a single fee line. One quote may show a bridge fee separately; another may account for costs in the output amount. The total to estimate is source gas + bridge or relayer fee + destination gas + pool fees and price impact on each swap leg.

An Arbitrum-to-Base route shows where the costs land

Say you send USDC from Arbitrum and receive ETH on Base. The route may charge Arbitrum gas to start, a bridge or relayer fee to move the value, then a Base pool fee and price impact when USDC is swapped for ETH, plus Base gas for the destination transaction.

For a small transfer, destination gas can take a bigger share of the received value than it does on a larger one, particularly when the destination is Ethereum mainnet rather than Base. Pool price impact also depends on the trade size and available liquidity, so the cheapest-looking fee breakdown may still produce less ETH.

For everyday route selection, compare the amount that arrives for the same input, then weigh that against the chains and timing you can accept. KyberSwap’s route quote is useful only as a snapshot: check the received amount before signing, since gas, liquidity and provider charges can change before execution.
