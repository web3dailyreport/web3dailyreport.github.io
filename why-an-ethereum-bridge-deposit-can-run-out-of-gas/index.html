Why an Ethereum Bridge Deposit Can Run Out of Gas

A bridge deposit needs enough ETH to cover its transfer and source-chain gas; token approvals and failed transactions can add costs before funds arrive.

Blockfront Editorial

Oct 24, 2026 · 18:28 UTC3 min read

An Ethereum bridge deposit needs ETH for network fees on top of the amount being moved, and a token deposit may require a separate approval transaction. If the sending wallet cannot cover the fee, Ethereum will not process the transaction; if a submitted transaction runs out of gas, the transfer can fail while the fee is still charged.

What does “short on gas” mean during a bridge deposit?

It means the sending account cannot cover the transaction’s fee at the gas price set for the Ethereum network. Ethereum calculates the fee from the gas used and the applicable fee per unit, so the ETH needed varies with network conditions and the work the bridge transaction performs.

The deposit amount and the fee are separate: a wallet holding exactly the tokens or ETH to bridge may still lack the ETH needed to send them. A Polygon Bridge deposit walkthrough covers the route in more detail; the same basic fee check applies before a source-chain transaction can proceed.

Why can a token deposit cost more?

An ERC-20 token contract can require the wallet to approve the bridge contract to move tokens. That approval is a separate Ethereum transaction, so it can incur its own fee before the deposit transaction is sent. Some interfaces may show an approval already in place, but the wallet should still be checked for enough ETH to cover each required transaction.

ETH deposits often avoid that token-approval step, but the bridge contract still has to process the deposit. More contract work can use more gas than a simple transfer. A wallet’s fee estimate is a guide, not a fixed price: the fee can change before the transaction is included.

What should you do if a deposit stalls?

Check the wallet and bridge status before sending again. A transaction awaiting confirmation may still complete; submitting a duplicate deposit can move funds twice. If the transaction failed, Ethereum’s transaction rules mean its state changes are reverted, but gas already used is still charged.

  • Check the sending wallet’s ETH balance, including room for the fee.
  • Review whether the bridge asks for token approval before deposit.
  • Use the transaction hash to check whether Ethereum marks it pending, confirmed or failed.
  • After confirmation, check the bridge’s own status for the destination transfer.

A low balance before submission usually means adding ETH for fees and retrying once. A confirmed source transaction with no destination funds is a different issue: follow the bridge’s status and recovery instructions rather than resending the deposit.
