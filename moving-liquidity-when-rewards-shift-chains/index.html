Moving Liquidity When Rewards Shift Chains

When incentives move chains, liquidity providers must unwind the old position, bridge assets and rebuild on the new network after comparing net costs and risks.

Blockfront Editorial

Oct 17, 2026 · 02:40 UTC3 min read

When a liquidity program moves its rewards to another chain, providers usually need to withdraw from the old pool, move the assets and deposit into a new one. The reward change itself does not transfer a position; the provider must compare what the new incentives pay against the costs and risks of moving.

What has to move when a pool changes chains?

A provider first removes liquidity from the original pool and receives the pool’s underlying tokens, subject to the pool’s rules and current balances. The provider then bridges supported assets to the destination chain and supplies them to a pool there; the tokens, pool contract and network are separate parts of the move.

Ethereum.org’s bridge guide explains that bridges transfer assets between networks, sometimes by creating a wrapped representation on the destination chain. For a closer look at route selection and cross-chain swaps, this guide to choosing a biswap route for a cross-chain move covers the details behind that step.

How do you decide if the new rewards are worth it?

Compare the expected reward with the full cost of repositioning and the risks of the new pool. A headline reward rate alone leaves out bridge fees, swap fees, gas on both networks, pool trading fees and the time funds may sit out of the market.

Before moving, check:

  • Whether the destination pool accepts the same tokens or requires a swap.
  • How much value remains after bridge, swap and transaction fees.
  • Whether rewards are paid in a liquid token and under what conditions.
  • How the new pool’s token balance and price exposure differ from the old one.

For most providers, the better choice is the pool with the strongest expected net return for a risk level they accept, not the largest advertised incentive. Liquidity positions can lose value relative to simply holding the tokens when prices move, and rewards may change while funds are deposited.

What should you check before bridging?

Confirm the source and destination networks, token contracts and receiving pool before signing transactions. Ethereum.org notes that bridges add smart-contract and, in some designs, counterparty risks; a bridge can also create a wrapped asset rather than move the original token itself.

Move a small amount first if the route or destination token is unfamiliar, and keep enough of the destination chain’s native asset for transaction fees. After the new deposit confirms, verify the position and reward eligibility in the destination protocol; the next step is to monitor whether net rewards still justify keeping liquidity there.
