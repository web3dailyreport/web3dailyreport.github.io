What happens during a transfer: Ethereum locks, Gnosis mints

What happens during a Gnosis Bridge transfer? Ethereum USDC is locked, validators attest to an AMB message, and Gnosis Chain mints tokens to the recipient.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 19, 2026 · 22:59 UTC2 min read

Gnosis Bridge moves an approved USDC deposit from Ethereum to Gnosis Chain by locking the tokens, relaying a validator-signed message through the Arbitrary Message Bridge (AMB), then minting tokens to the recipient at the signature threshold. The project’s Omnibridge documentation describes this lock-and-mint flow. Before sending, use Gnosis Bridge to access the service for moving assets between Ethereum and Gnosis Chain.

Gnosis Bridge locks USDC before validators sign its AMB message

Take an illustrative case: a user wants to move USDC from Ethereum to their own address on Gnosis Chain. They first approve the Omnibridge contract to use the token, then call that contract to make the deposit.

Omnibridge locks the USDC on Ethereum and sends a message through the AMB. That message carries the information needed to process the transfer on Gnosis Chain. The tokens are not yet in the recipient’s Gnosis Chain wallet while this message awaits confirmation.

Bridge validators wait for the Ethereum deposit to reach the required confirmations. Each validator then submits a signature on Gnosis Chain to attest to the message. The number of confirmations and the time this stage takes can vary; the key event is the signatures reaching the bridge’s threshold.

At that threshold, the AMB calls the Gnosis-side Omnibridge mediator, which mints the bridged USDC to the recipient. For this Ethereum-to-Gnosis transfer, execution happens automatically once enough validators sign; the recipient does not need to make a separate claim.

The first USDC transfer creates its Gnosis Chain token representation

On a token’s first-ever Omnibridge transfer, Omnibridge deploys a representation contract for it on Gnosis Chain and mints the bridged amount there. Later transfers of that same token use the existing contract and mint additional tokens into it.

That distinction explains why the asset arriving on Gnosis Chain is a bridged representation, rather than USDC held at its Ethereum contract address. In the illustrative case, the user’s deposit moves through three observable stages: USDC locked on Ethereum, validator signatures recorded on Gnosis Chain, and bridged USDC minted to their destination address.

For your own deposit, identify which stage it has reached before deciding what to do next: check the Ethereum transaction for the lock, allow time for validator signatures, then look for the minted token on Gnosis Chain. Once it appears at the destination, the transfer is complete.
