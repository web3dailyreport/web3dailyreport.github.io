US moves to forfeit $1.1 billion from Binance hack

The Justice Department filed to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC tied to Binance’s October hack, as US-led efforts have frozen, seized or returned $2.9 billion.

Blockfront Editorial

Nov 25, 2026 · 10:39 UTC2 min read

The Justice Department filed on Nov. 23 to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC linked to the theft from Binance, a step toward recovering assets from the October hack. The department said in its forfeiture announcement that the funds had already been subject to a government-requested restraining order.

The civil filing targets USDC, a stablecoin issued by Circle Internet Financial. The department said the tokens are proceeds of the Binance theft or funds laundered from it; the filing seeks forfeiture, so the claim is now before the court rather than a completed transfer to victims.

The theft occurred on or about Oct. 29. The Justice Department said North Korean cyber actors stole more than $6 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance, and that US government and private-sector efforts have since helped freeze, seize or return more than $2.9 billion.

That broader total includes funds frozen by private companies as well as assets seized or returned, according to the department. On Nov. 12, officials said the government had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of over $2.7 billion then frozen. The Associated Press reported that forfeiture claims are typically reviewed by a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The FBI, U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are investigating, the Justice Department said. The FBI said it and its partners traced and froze assets and shared information with virtual asset service providers. Circle assisted the investigation.

The department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets are continuing. It said the attackers have moved funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.
